Independent Software Engineering

Forever Body Fitness website & Sanity integration

  • React
  • TypeScript
  • Sanity
  • GROQ
  • Portable Text
  • Heroku

I independently built the Forever Body Fitness website, originally called Tedashi Trained, and connected its React frontend to a separate Sanity backend for publishing and organizing content.

Outcome
The deployed site lets editors maintain posts, categories, and featured content through Sanity without a code change for routine updates.
Read the Forever Body Fitness case study
Publishing and visitor requirements
The business needed to create and edit posts with featured images and rich formatting, choose which posts to highlight across the site or within a category, and let visitors browse and filter content across screen sizes. Those requirements shaped the Sanity content model, GROQ queries, and React rendering logic.
My role
I developed the React and TypeScript site, connected it to Sanity, implemented the publishing behavior in the frontend, and configured and validated the production deployment on Heroku.
Content architecture
I worked with Sanity schemas and references for categories, featured posts, images, and structured content. GROQ retrieves the relevant documents and references from the separate CMS backend, and the frontend uses Portable Text to render rich content, including formatting and images.
Category and featured-post logic
I implemented global and category-specific pins. A category pin appears only in its own category. I also implemented category filtering, hid unused categories, and ordered the remaining posts by publication date.
Production configuration and verification
I worked through the differences between the local frontend and the Heroku deployment. I configured the production environment variables and allowed the deployed frontend’s origin in Sanity’s CORS settings, then verified that the live site could retrieve and render managed content.

Cloud Architecture & AWS Engineering

BCSI in the Cloud

  • CloudFormation
  • VPC
  • IAM
  • KMS
  • S3
  • EC2
  • CloudTrail
  • Athena

I built a deployable AWS workshop lab so energy and utility partners could work with infrastructure, security controls, and audit evidence for a cloud migration.

Outcome

I delivered the workshop six times, with the content recording more than 1,300 views.

The engagement also helped secure a commitment from a major U.S. energy-sector provider to migrate an existing on-premises BCSI data protection solution to AWS.

This demonstrated how hands-on technical enablement and cloud architecture guidance could support modernization of established data protection solutions in a highly regulated industry.

Read the BCSI case study
Challenge
Partners evaluating AWS for workloads involving Bulk Electric System (BES) Cyber System Information (BCSI) needed to see how cloud infrastructure, access, encryption, and audit evidence fit together.
My role
I developed the workshop and its deployable environment, built CloudTrail and Athena reporting patterns, and delivered the lab to partner and customer audiences.
How the lab worked
CloudFormation provisioned the environment. Amazon VPC and EC2 provided networking and compute. IAM and AWS KMS addressed access and encryption, and Amazon S3 provided storage. CloudTrail recorded AWS activity and Athena let participants query audit information and examine evidence.
Technical decisions
CloudFormation gave participants a repeatable environment for exploring the relationships between services. With the lab deployed, they could inspect the infrastructure and work through the audit evidence themselves.

AWS Partner Engineering & Co-Building

Tracing a missing AWS Marketplace email

  • AWS Marketplace
  • CloudFormation
  • Amazon SNS

I helped a partner trace a missing validation email to the Amazon SNS notification path and guided a topic configuration change.

Outcome
Email delivery resumed. The listing used for troubleshooting passed validation, followed by the other four.
Read the Marketplace troubleshooting case study
Observed symptom
The partner had five listings to validate. A required email repeatedly failed to arrive, blocking completion of the validation workflow.
Scope isolation
During a screen share, I worked with the partner to isolate one listing. That gave us a single integration and notification path to follow before validating the other listings.
Integration and log review
I examined the listing’s CloudFormation integration with the partner. We reviewed relevant logs and followed the expected notification path across the AWS services involved, which narrowed our attention to Amazon Simple Notification Service (SNS) and its topic configuration.
Partner-led remediation
I guided the partner through the remediation. The partner made the SNS topic configuration change in its own environment.
Validation
We confirmed that the previously missing email arrived after the change and that the listing we investigated passed the original validation workflow. The remaining four listings then passed validation.

  • AWS Well-Architected
  • Technical assessment
  • Reviewer calibration

I co-led a redesign of technical partner reviews and wrote calibration guides that helped reviewers apply the same assessment criteria.

Outcome

Read the validation modernization case study
Challenge

The redesigned process reduced review time and improved consistency across reviews. The guides and enablement sessions were my deliverables within the broader team effort.

Technical Content & Enablement · AWS Core Compute

AWS Compute Pricing & Cost Optimization — First-Call Technical Enablement

I developed and delivered a reusable first-call presentation to help AWS sellers and Solutions Architects explain EC2 purchasing options, cost tradeoffs, and capacity considerations.

Read the compute pricing case study
Challenge
Early customer and partner conversations often conflated pricing discounts with capacity guarantees. Sellers and Solutions Architects needed a technically accurate resource that explained commitment requirements and helped them identify which options fit a workload.
My contribution
As part of the AWS Core Compute Technical Field Community, I structured and wrote the presentation, identified common misconceptions, and worked with AWS Compute and Capacity product teams to validate the technical explanations. I wrote detailed speaker notes so colleagues could deliver the material independently.
Technical approach
I organized the material around customer decisions about flexibility, predictable usage, and financial commitments. I explained the tradeoffs between Savings Plans and Reserved Instances, then covered Capacity Reservations and how capacity assurance differs from discounted usage. The material helped sellers narrow the purchasing options and identify questions that needed specialist input.
Outcome
I presented the material to more than 60 people. Colleagues within and outside Core Compute adopted it as a reusable resource for introductory conversations, giving teams consistent guidance they could deliver themselves.

Cloud Architecture & AWS Engineering

Simplifying an AWS Compute Architecture with EC2 Auto Scaling Instance Refresh

I helped a technology partner evaluate a simpler way to replace application instances while maintaining service availability, then demonstrated an alternative using native EC2 Auto Scaling Instance Refresh.

Read the Instance Refresh case study
Challenge
The partner’s CloudFormation-managed design used two EC2 Auto Scaling groups, two load-balancer target groups, and custom AWS Lambda orchestration to coordinate instance replacement and maintain application capacity.
Discovery
I reviewed what each component was intended to do and investigated why native Instance Refresh had been ruled out. The key requirement was to replace instances while preserving application availability and behavior.
Alternative design
I evaluated how Instance Refresh could replace instances incrementally within an Auto Scaling group. I identified how useful existing Lambda functionality could stay in the design while the AWS-managed capability took over instance replacement.
Demonstrated outcome
I demonstrated an architecture that met the instance-replacement requirements with fewer components and less reliance on duplicate capacity and custom orchestration.
Technologies
Amazon EC2, EC2 Auto Scaling Instance Refresh, AWS CloudFormation, AWS Lambda, Application Load Balancer, and load-balancer target groups.
